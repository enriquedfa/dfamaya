Capsule privacy policy

Effective date: 20 November 2026

Capsule is a web browser for Android made by Enrique Amaya. This policy explains what Capsule keeps on your phone, what leaves it, how your data is kept safe, and how to delete it. For any question about privacy, write to developerdfa@gmail.com.

In short

  • Capsule has no account, no sync, no ads, no analytics and no crash reporter of its own. The developer receives none of your data.
  • Your history, tabs, pins, bookmarks, settings and the things you hide on pages stay on your phone.
  • The websites you open see what any website sees when you visit it. That is browsing, and what each site does with it is up to that site.
  • Apart from the pages you open, Capsule itself sends only what is listed under What Capsule itself sends, and most of it can be turned off.

The websites you open

When you open a page, your phone connects to that website and to whatever the page loads, as in any browser. Those sites see your phone’s internet (IP) address, the page you asked for, and what you type into them, and they can set cookies. Their own privacy policies apply.

Capsule cuts down what they learn:

  • Ads and trackers on Capsule’s block lists never load, so those companies never hear from your phone. You can pause blocking for a site.
  • Tracking codes are removed from the links you open, unless you turn “Clean tracking from links” off in Settings.
  • Encrypted connections: Capsule asks for the secure (HTTPS) version of every site and warns you before opening a site that doesn’t have one.
  • Private tabs keep no history, and their cookies and site data are deleted when the last private tab closes.
  • Your location, camera and microphone reach a site only if you allow that site, in Capsule’s own prompt. Capsule remembers your answer for that site only, and you can change it in Settings › Website settings. In a private tab you are asked each time.

Pictures in Reader. Reader shows an article in Capsule’s own layout, so Capsule downloads the article’s pictures itself, from the sites the page names. Each picture is first checked against the block lists, and the request carries no cookies and no referrer.

Protected video and music. Some film and music services protect their streams (DRM). To play them, Android gives that site an identifier for your phone, specific to that site. Capsule sends nothing for it. It is on by default, off in private tabs, and Settings › Privacy › Protected video and music turns it off everywhere (those services then won’t play).

What Capsule itself sends

This is the complete list of what Capsule sends on its own, as opposed to the pages you open. All of it goes over encrypted connections.

Search suggestions

While you type in the address bar, Capsule sends what you have typed so far to the search engine you chose (DuckDuckGo unless you change it), to show suggestions. The request carries your typing and nothing else: no cookies and no identifier. The search engine handles it under its own privacy policy. Turn it off in Settings › Search › Search suggestions.

Google Play’s Data safety section lists this as “In-app search history”: collected for app functionality, optional, and not shared.

Suggestions from sites

When you search inside a site from the address bar (its name shows as a chip there), and that site publishes search suggestions (Wikipedia, Wiktionary and MDN do), Capsule sends what you have typed so far to that site, to show its suggestions. As with the search engine, the request carries your typing and nothing else: no cookies and no identifier. It is never sent from a private tab. The site handles it under its own privacy policy. Turn it off in Settings › Privacy › Suggestions from sites.

Block-list updates

Every six hours, on Wi-Fi or another unmetered connection, Capsule downloads fresh copies of its block lists from the lists’ own websites (EasyList, Fanboy, uBlock Origin’s and AdGuard’s lists, and a regional list for your phone’s languages). The requests carry nothing about you or your browsing. Turn it off in Settings › Privacy › Update automatically, and Capsule uses the copy built into the app.

Translation languages

Translate works on your phone: the page’s words never leave it. The first time you translate from or into a language, Capsule downloads that language’s data (about 40 MB) from Google, through Google’s ML Kit. Capsule switches off ML Kit’s usage reporting, so Google receives no usage information from Capsule. You can delete downloaded languages in Settings › Translate.

Safe Browsing

Android’s web engine (Android System WebView) checks the sites you visit against Google Safe Browsing, to warn you before a page known to be dangerous. It is part of WebView, made by Google, and Google handles it under the Google Privacy Policy.

Summarise and Ask

On phones that have it, Summarise and Ask about this page use Gemini Nano, a model that runs on your phone through Android’s AICore. Capsule gives the model the page’s text only when you tap Summarise or ask a question, and that text, your questions and the answers never leave your phone. Android downloads and updates the model itself, for every app that uses it.

What Android and Google Play do

  • WebView’s crash reports. If you have chosen to share usage and diagnostics with Google in your phone’s settings, Android System WebView may send Google a crash report when WebView itself crashes. Google says these reports contain no web addresses (WebView and privacy). Capsule switches off WebView’s usage statistics for everyone; the crash reports are only stopped by that phone setting.
  • Updates. Google Play updates Capsule, and Android System WebView, as it does every app.

What Capsule keeps on your phone

  • History of the pages you visited (not in private tabs), your open tabs and recently closed tabs.
  • Pins, and the icons of the pinned sites.
  • Bookmarks, their spaces and folders, and the icons of bookmarks you import.
  • Sites you can search from the address bar, learned from the search boxes you use on them (not in private tabs).
  • Your settings, your settings for each site, the things you hid on sites, and your answers to sites’ permission requests.
  • Cookies and site data, kept by WebView, as in any browser, so you stay signed in.
  • Downloads you start, which Android saves to your Downloads folder.
  • Translation languages you downloaded.

All of this is in Capsule’s private storage, which other apps can’t read, except your downloads, which are files you keep.

Other apps

  • Links you open from other apps come to Capsule from that app, which already knows the link. When an app opens a link in a Capsule tab over itself (a Custom Tab), Capsule tells that app nothing about what you do there.
  • When you share a page or open a link in another app, it goes to the app you pick, because you asked.
  • Finding the app that owns a link. To show an installed app’s icon in the bar, name it in prompts before leaving Capsule, and offer “Open in [App]” in Page info and the link menu, Capsule asks Android which installed app handles the current address (using Android’s QUERY_ALL_PACKAGES permission, as other web browsers do). That lookup runs only on your phone, is never stored or sent anywhere, and is never exposed to websites.

Sharing

Capsule doesn’t sell or rent your data, and it shares it with no one beyond what this page describes.

How your data is kept safe

  • Everything Capsule keeps is in its private storage on your phone.
  • What Capsule itself sends goes only over encrypted connections (HTTPS), and only to the services named above.
  • Android’s cloud backup and phone-to-phone transfer copy nothing of Capsule’s.

Keeping and deleting your data

  • History stays until you delete it, the pages you select or all of it, in History. Clearing history also forgets the sites learned for searching from the address bar in that time.
  • Bookmarks stay until you delete them, in Bookmarks.
  • A site’s settings, hidden things and permissions go with Settings › Website settings › Forget this site, or Forget all sites.
  • Everything goes when you uninstall Capsule or clear its storage in Android’s Settings. Your downloads stay in your Downloads folder until you delete them.
  • The developer holds no copy of your data, so there is nothing to ask them to delete. Websites and search engines keep what they log under their own policies.

Children

Capsule is not directed at children.
