Brief

Privacy policy

Last updated: 30 October 2026

Brief is a Wear OS “at a glance” app: a watch complication, a tile, and matching widgets (a phone home-screen widget and, on Wear OS 7, a watch widget) that show the single most relevant thing right now — your now-playing music, an alert from an app you choose, your next calendar event, a low phone-battery warning, the current weather, a scheduled reminder, or today’s date. It comes in two parts: the watch app (which only displays what it’s told) and the phone companion (which gathers the information, sends it to your watch, and shows the same glance in its own widget).

This policy explains exactly what Brief accesses, why, and where it goes. The short version: Brief has no servers, no account, no analytics, and no ads. Almost everything stays on your own devices. The only piece of your information Brief itself sends to a third party is an approximate location — rounded to about a kilometre — to a weather provider so it can return your local forecast. Everything else — including whatever is shown as a glance — only ever travels between your own phone and your paired watch.

Google Play handles three things on the app’s behalf — a voluntary tip, its own rating card, and app updates — but none of those carries anything Brief has gathered about you, and Brief never sees your payment details. See Google Play services below.

There is one thing you can choose to send: if you use “Send feedback” and tick Attach diagnostics, Brief prepares a file describing what it was doing and attaches it to an email you write and send yourself. Nothing is transmitted unless you tick that box and send that email — and the detailed logging that file can carry is itself a setting that is off by default. See “Send feedback” and diagnostics below.

Who is responsible

Brief is an independent app. The developer is the “data controller” for the purposes of this policy. Questions or requests: developerdfa@gmail.com.

Notification access is used for two things: to read the active media session (so Brief can show now-playing and forward play/pause), and — only if you turn on the Notifications source and choose which apps count — to mirror those selected apps’ alerts to your watch. Brief looks only at the apps you pick; notifications from every other app are ignored, never stored, and never uploaded. If you don’t enable the Notifications source, notification access is used for media alone.

What leaves your device

  • Approximate location → weather provider. When weather is enabled, Brief sends coordinates to Open-Meteo (open-meteo.com) over an encrypted (HTTPS) connection to retrieve the forecast. The coordinates are rounded to about a kilometre before they leave the phone, so your street-level position is never sent, and Brief keeps no location history. Open-Meteo requires no account or API key. See Open-Meteo’s own privacy terms at https://open-meteo.com/en/terms. If you set a manual location instead of using your phone’s location, only that manual location is sent. You can disable weather entirely.
  • A location search you type (when choosing a manual location) is sent to Open-Meteo’s geocoding service to turn the place name into coordinates.

Apart from that, Brief sends none of the information it gathers off your devices on its own. There is no Brief server — calendar titles, media details, the alerts you choose to mirror, reminders, and settings are never uploaded to the developer or any third party by the app. (Your settings file can be included in your own device’s Android Backup — a system feature under your Google account that the developer has no access to; see “Where data is stored” below.)

Two other things do cross the app’s boundary, and neither carries what Brief has gathered about you:

  • A feedback email you write and send yourself — entirely under your control and never automatic. Described next.
  • Google Play’s own purchase, rating and update flows, which are about the app rather than about you. See Google Play services.

“Send feedback” and diagnostics

Settings → Send feedback opens your email app so you can write to the developer. Before it does, it asks whether to attach a diagnostics file. Both parts of that choice are yours:

  • Leave “Attach diagnostics” unticked and the email carries only what it always has: your app version, Android version, and device model, plus whatever you type.
  • Tick it and Brief builds a .zip on your phone and attaches it to that email.

What the attachment contains. App and watch versions, device models, OS versions, locale and time zone; which permissions are granted; whether your watch is connected and running the watch app; your settings; what Brief and your watch are showing right now and why; and — only if you have switched on the logging setting described below — a recent timeline of what Brief resolved, sent to your watch, and displayed, including, when the box below is ticked, the calendar titles, track names, and reminder and notification text that appeared in those glances.

“Include titles and text” is a second, nested tick box. Untick it and every piece of that content is replaced by its length ([redacted:7 chars]) before the file is written, and the free text in your settings — reminder text, the keywords you hide, your custom fallback message, and any custom weather coordinates — is replaced the same way. The timeline itself is kept, which is usually enough to diagnose a problem without it.

How it is sent. Brief writes the file to its own temporary storage and hands it to whichever email app you pick, which is granted permission to read that one file. Brief does not upload it, and does not send the email. Nothing leaves your phone unless you press send in your email app, and you can open the zip and read it first — it is plain text. Once sent, the email is in your outbox and the developer’s inbox like any other email you write; it is used only to investigate what you reported, and is not shared, published, or used for anything else. Files Brief prepared are deleted from its temporary storage within a day, and are never backed up.

Asking your watch. When you tick the box, your phone asks your paired watch for its side of the picture — what it currently has cached and why it is showing what it is showing. That request travels only between your own paired devices, exactly like every other glance, and happens only at that moment. If your watch is off or out of range, the file is built without it.

The log itself — off unless you turn it on. So that an attachment can describe a problem that has already happened, Brief can keep a small local log of its own decisions: what the calendar query found, what was sent to the watch, what the watch displayed or hid, what a music button asked your music app to do (the app’s package name and the command, never the track), and why. This is a setting — Settings → Log diagnostics data — and it is off by default. Nothing is recorded until you switch it on.

While it is on, the log lives in each app’s private storage on your phone and your watch, is capped at roughly 190 KB per device (the oldest entries are discarded as new ones arrive), is excluded from every backup and device transfer, and is deleted when you uninstall Brief. It is never transmitted anywhere on its own — it exists solely so that, if you choose to send a report, there is something to attach.

Turning it off deletes it. Switching the setting off erases what has been recorded — on your phone immediately, and on your watch as soon as the change reaches it. If you never turn it on, an attachment still works; it just describes what Brief is doing at that moment rather than what led up to it.

Google Play services

Brief is distributed through Google Play and uses three of Play’s own flows. All three are run by the Play Store on your device — Brief hands off to it and gets back only the result described below. None of them sends Google, or the developer, anything Brief has gathered about you: no calendar events, no notification content, no now-playing details, no location, no settings.

  • The tip jar (Google Play Billing). Settings → Support development offers a voluntary one-time tip. The payment happens entirely inside Google Play’s own purchase sheet: Brief never sees, asks for, or stores your payment method, card number, billing address, or Google account. What Brief gets back from Play is the tier names and prices to display (localized by Play) and, after a tip, a purchase token it immediately hands back to Play to finalize the purchase. Brief keeps no record of the tip — there is no “has tipped” flag anywhere in the app, because a tip deliberately unlocks nothing: every feature is identical before and after, and there is nothing in the app to buy. The only lasting record is the one Google Play keeps in your own purchase history. Brief also contacts the on-device Play Billing service once per app launch to finalize any tip that completed while the app was closed (Play automatically refunds a purchase the app never finalizes); if you have never tipped, that check finds nothing and there is no purchase data to see.
  • The rating card (Play In-App Review). Now and then Brief asks Play to show its own in-app rating card. Anything you type or rate there goes to Google Play, not to Brief: the API returns no result, so Brief cannot tell whether you rated, what you said, or even whether the card was shown — only whether the request itself failed. To avoid nagging, Brief keeps three numbers on your phone only — how many times you have opened the app, when you first opened it, and when it last asked — and asks only after onboarding is done, at least 5 opens spread over at least 7 days, and at most once every 90 days. Settings → Rate Brief is the deterministic alternative: it just opens Brief’s Play Store listing.
  • Update prompts (Play In-App Update). While the app is open, Brief asks Play whether a newer version of Brief exists and, if one does, lets Play offer its “flexible” update — the download runs in the background while the app stays usable, and Brief offers a restart once it is ready. That is a question about the app’s version number; it carries nothing about you.

None of these is required to use Brief, and on a device without Google Play (or a build installed from outside it) all three simply do nothing.

The phone↔watch link described next also runs on Google Play services, but it is a separate, device-to-device channel — see Between your phone and your watch.

Between your phone and your watch

Brief’s phone companion sends the resolved glance, your settings, and media play/pause commands to your paired watch over the Wear OS Data Layer (provided by Google Play Services). This travels directly between your own paired devices over their encrypted Bluetooth/Wi-Fi connection. It is not routed through a Brief server.

When the Notifications source is on, the resolved glance can include a selected app’s name, the notification’s title and text, and — while “Show app icon” is on — the app’s own status bar icon, so the watch can show the mark you already recognise instead of a generic bell. That icon is artwork supplied by the app itself; it carries none of your content, and turning the setting off stops Brief from reading it at all. Like every other glance, this goes only to your paired watch and never to a server. On the phone, the winning glance — including any mirrored notification content — is kept in Brief’s private app storage so the home-screen widget can render it; it is excluded from every backup, replaced as soon as a different glance wins (for example, when you dismiss the alert or its show window ends), and deleted when you uninstall Brief. On Samsung phones you can also place Brief on the lock screen and Always On Display, which anyone holding the phone can read: by default that widget shows only the notifying app’s name and “Busy” for an event, and the Hide details on the lock screen switch in Widget appearance is what lets it show the text itself.

If you turn on “mask titles,” event titles and locations are replaced with a generic placeholder before they ever leave the phone, so the real text is never sent to the watch.

When the Automation source is on, the resolved glance can include the title, text and task name an automation app on your phone sent Brief; it goes only to your paired watch, like every other glance. The access key that lets an app send a glance is generated on your phone and stays there: it is never synced to the watch, never included in a settings backup or export, and never part of a diagnostics bundle. Regenerating it in Brief’s settings revokes every app using the old one.

Where data is stored, and for how long

  • All data Brief keeps lives on-device in private app storage (Android DataStore): the last resolved glance (kept on the phone for the widget and on the watch for the complication and tile), a cached weather forecast, and your settings.
  • The cached weather forecast and now-playing details expire automatically (e.g. stale weather and paused/old media are dropped within minutes to hours).
  • Mirrored notification content exists only inside the last resolved glance. When a selected app’s alert is the winning glance, its app name, title, and text are stored as part of that glance in the phone’s and the watch’s private app storage — nowhere else. It is replaced as soon as a different glance wins (for example when the alert is dismissed, its show window ends, or you turn notification access off), it is never backed up or transferred to a new device, and uninstalling Brief deletes it.
  • Your settings take part in Android Backup, so a new or reset phone comes back set up the way you left it. One file is backed up: Brief’s settings file — the same settings document the export below writes, so it carries the same things (including a manual weather location, if you set one), plus a few local app-state values: whether onboarding is finished, whether you dismissed a one-time hint, the counters that decide when Play’s rating card may appear, and two housekeeping entries — when Brief last synced to your watch, and which source won that glance. Nothing else goes into the backup — no glance content, not the weather cache or its coordinates, not the diagnostics log. Cloud copies are made only on devices that can end-to-end encrypt them with your screen lock, are stored under your own Google account, and are never visible to the developer. Backup is controlled by Android’s system Backup setting, which you can turn off at any time.
  • You can also export your settings to a file you choose (Settings → Backup and restore). The file is a plain JSON copy of Brief’s settings document, written to the location you pick and read back when you restore — so it contains exactly what that document holds, and nothing else: priority order, source toggles, units, the reminders you created (their text included), the calendars and apps you selected, the keywords you hide, your custom fallback text, and — if you chose a manual weather location instead of using your phone’s location — that place’s name and its coordinates, saved exactly as you picked them rather than rounded, because there they are a setting rather than a reading. What the file cannot contain is anything that never enters that settings document: no event content, no now-playing details, no mirrored notification content, no location fix from your phone’s location services, and no weather cache. Brief has no access to the file beyond the moment you pick it, and where it ends up is entirely your choice — if you save it to a cloud folder or send it to another device, a manual weather location travels with it, and that provider’s terms apply to it.
  • The diagnostics log (see “Send feedback” and diagnostics) exists only while you have Settings → Log diagnostics data switched on — it is off by default. While on, it lives in each app’s private storage on the phone and on the watch, is capped at roughly 190 KB per device with the oldest entries discarded as new ones arrive, is erased when you switch the setting off, and is deleted when you uninstall Brief. Any diagnostics file you asked Brief to prepare is written to temporary storage and removed within a day. Neither is backed up or transferred to a new device.
  • Everything else stays out of the backup. The cached weather forecast and its location coordinates, the last resolved glance, any mirrored notification content, the diagnostics log, and the watch’s caches are excluded from Android cloud backup and device-to-device transfer.
  • Uninstalling Brief removes all of this on-device data.

Data sharing and sale

Brief does not sell your data, share it for advertising, or use it for any purpose other than showing your glance — and that includes the alerts mirrored by the Notifications source, which only ever reach your own watch. There are no third-party analytics, advertising, or tracking SDKs in the app, and no automatic crash, usage, or diagnostic reporting of any kind. The only third party that receives any of your data from the app is the weather provider (Open-Meteo), and only the approximate location needed to return a forecast. Google Play mediates three things — a voluntary tip, its own rating card, and app updates — but what passes there is about the app and your own Play purchase, never anything Brief has gathered about you; see Google Play services.

If you choose to email a diagnostics file to the developer, its contents are used solely to investigate the problem you reported and are not shared with anyone else — see “Send feedback” and diagnostics. You can ask for it to be deleted at any time at the contact address below.

Permissions, in plain terms

  • Calendar (read-only): read your upcoming events to show the next one. Brief never creates, edits, or deletes your events.
  • Location (approximate, including in the background): fetch local weather. Brief rounds your coordinates to about a kilometre before sending them and keeps no location history. Background access lets the forecast refresh even while the app is closed; you can avoid it by setting a manual location or disabling weather.
  • Notification access: read the active media session for now-playing and transport controls, and — when you enable the Notifications source — mirror alerts from the apps you choose. The same single grant covers both, and Brief reads notifications only from the apps you pick.
  • Do Not Disturb (read-only): check whether your phone is in Do Not Disturb so Brief can mute glances while it’s on. Reading this state needs no permission grant, and Brief never changes your Do Not Disturb settings.
  • Exact alarms / run at startup: wake the companion at the right moments (e.g. when an event is about to start) so the glance is timely, and restore scheduling after a reboot. Brief may also point you to Android’s battery-optimization settings so background refresh isn’t delayed, but it does not change them for you.

The low-battery source reads your phone’s own battery level locally and needs no permission, as does the charge-time estimate the phone-battery glance floor shows while you are plugged in.

You can revoke any of these in Android Settings at any time; Brief degrades gracefully (the affected source simply stops appearing).

Children

Brief is not directed at children and does not knowingly collect data from children.

Security

Data stays in private, app-scoped on-device storage; network requests use HTTPS; and the phone↔watch link relies on the platform’s encrypted, pairing-authenticated channel.
